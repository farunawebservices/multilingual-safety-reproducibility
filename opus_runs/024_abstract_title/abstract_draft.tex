\begin{abstract}
Safety behaviour in language models is usually measured on single English prompts answered by a single model, while deployed systems are multilingual and agentic, distributing decisions across roles such as planners and critics. Evidence on activation steering is correspondingly narrow: mostly English, often single-model, and reported without stated multiple-comparison families. We evaluate planner-targeted activation steering on a shared Arabic, English, Hausa, and Yoruba prompt set across four open-weight models, with two closed models included only as descriptive baselines. Comparisons are prompt-matched within model and tested with the exact paired McNemar test. Each model entry and outcome carries its own pre-declared, disjoint Holm family; harmful and benign arms are never combined; partial coverage is reported at exact recorded denominators; six clearly labelled exploratory additions declare no correction family. Within their families, harmful operational failure fell substantially under steering for Llama and Qwen in several languages, GPT-OSS moved in mixed directions---one language improved and one worsened---Gemma showed no family-corrected change, and the Llama benign refusal arm was null at the floor. The closed-model results are descriptive only. Per-model, per-language measurement is therefore required; these contrasts license no general claim about steering.
\end{abstract}
