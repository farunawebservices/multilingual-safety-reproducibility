\subsection{Claude Sonnet 4 vs 5 cross-version comparison}

The second closed-model baseline asks a different descriptive question: do veto
outcomes differ between two versions of the same closed model when both are
evaluated on shared Phase~1 prompts? The comparison covers Claude Sonnet~4 and
Claude Sonnet~5 jointly; no standalone Sonnet~4 or Sonnet~5 analysis exists in
the evidence base, and none is reported here. Both sides are Phase~1 records for
two separate model versions, with \emph{no} steering condition on either side,
so the model-version difference is the only contrast and there is no intervention
arm. The cells are Arabic, English, Hausa, and Yoruba crossed with benign and
harmful prompt classes. The outcome is veto status, defined as
\texttt{is\_vetoed = true}. Benign denominators are 150/150/150/45 and harmful
denominators are 300 in each language, over 1{,}695 shared prompt IDs, with 1
partial-coverage row (Yoruba benign, 45 shared prompts). The comparison is
prompt-matched on those shared IDs, and per-cell discordance counts are recorded
alongside the marginal rates. The metadata consistency audit
(\texttt{metrics/claude\_sonnet\_4\_vs\_5\_metadata\_consistency\_audit.csv},
1 row) reports 1{,}695 records present in both versions, with language and
harmfulness matching for all of them. Cell-level values are those recorded in
\texttt{metrics/claude\_sonnet\_4\_vs\_5\_outcome\_comparison.csv} (8 rows) and
displayed in
\texttt{tables/claude\_sonnet\_4\_vs\_5\_descriptive\_comparison.tex}, with
provenance in
\texttt{handoffs/claude\_sonnet\_4\_vs\_5\_OPUS\_HANDOFF.md} and
\texttt{manifests/claude\_sonnet\_4\_vs\_5\_artifact\_manifest.json}. The
manifest additionally declares
\texttt{claude\_sonnet\_4\_vs\_5\_paper\_table.csv} as the authoritative
displayed table, but that file is not in the packet and is not cited as evidence
here. No hypothesis test, no confidence interval, and no multiple-testing
correction is defined for or applied to any cell in this subsection.
Missing-label handling is not reported in the shipped CSVs, which contain no
parse or missing columns, so we make no missing-label statement for either
version.

All eight cells below are descriptive prompt-matched rates, differences in
percentage points, and agreement rates over shared prompt IDs. In all four
benign cells the two versions are identical: Arabic benign (150 shared prompts),
English benign (150), Hausa benign (150), and Yoruba benign (45 shared prompts)
each record 0 vetoes under Sonnet~4 and 0 vetoes under Sonnet~5, with 100.00\%
agreement in every cell. The four harmful cells, each with 300 shared prompts,
differ in both magnitude and direction. Arabic harmful records 23 vetoes
(7.67\%) under Sonnet~4 against 13 (4.33\%) under Sonnet~5, a difference of
$-3.33$~pp, with 20 prompts vetoed only by Sonnet~4 and 10 vetoed only by
Sonnet~5 and 90.00\% agreement. English harmful records 10 vetoes (3.33\%)
against 0 (0.00\%), a difference of $-3.33$~pp, with 10 discordant prompts
vetoed only by Sonnet~4 and 0 vetoed only by Sonnet~5, and 96.67\% agreement.
The two remaining harmful cells move in the opposite direction: Hausa harmful
records 2 vetoes (0.67\%) against 3 (1.00\%), a difference of $+0.33$~pp, with
98.33\% agreement; and Yoruba harmful records 2 vetoes (0.67\%) against 9
(3.00\%), a difference of $+2.33$~pp, with 96.33\% agreement. Over the full set
of shared prompts, the handoff and manifest record an overall agreement of
96.70\% of 1{,}695 records; we quote that figure as recorded and do not
recompute or re-derive it from the eight cells.

The reading of these eight cells is tightly bounded. They are descriptive: no
test, interval, or correction family is attached to them, and no statement in
this subsection may be read as inferential. They are also explicitly non-causal.
The contrast is between two model versions rather than between two conditions of
one model, so it is not a within-model steering effect and cannot be interpreted
as one; nothing here identifies what produced the differences between Sonnet~4
and Sonnet~5, and the version difference is not an intervention that we applied.
Because the analysis carries no correction family of its own, the Qwen
33-cell family and the Gemma three-cell family of Sections~4.2 and~4.3 are
neither applied to nor reported for these cells, and no cell is described as
significant or non-significant. The per-cell agreement rates and discordance
counts are reported exactly as recorded, the overall agreement figure is taken
from the handoff and manifest rather than aggregated from the cells, and the
Yoruba benign row is reported against its 45 shared prompts rather than any
larger nominal denominator. Finally, the two versions are identified only by the
strings ``Claude Sonnet 4'' and ``Claude Sonnet 5''; no vendor API string or
snapshot date is recorded in the evidence base, so these rates describe the
records collected under those two version labels and are not claimed to
characterise any particular vendor snapshot. As with the GPT-5 baseline, these
rates are not pooled with, contrasted against, or used to rank any other model
entry in this paper.
