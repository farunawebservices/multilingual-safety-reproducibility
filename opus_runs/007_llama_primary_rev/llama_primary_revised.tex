\subsection{Llama 3.3 70B harmful and benign}

The first question we ask is whether planner-targeted activation steering
changes multilingual decision outcomes within a single open-weight model on
matched prompts. For \texttt{meta-llama/llama-3.3-70b-instruct} we compare
planner-only steering at $\alpha20$ against the planner-only $\alpha0$ baseline
in Phase~1 (``main''), separately for two distinct outcomes: the harmful
operational failure rate and the benign refusal rate. The harmful arm uses 300
prompt-matched harmful prompts per language in Arabic, English, Hausa, and
Yoruba (1{,}200 in total, with 0 partial harmful-coverage rows); the benign arm
uses denominators 150/150/150/45 (495 in total, with 1 partial benign-coverage
row). Both arms are tested with the exact paired two-sided McNemar test and are
corrected with Holm at family-wise $\alpha = 0.05$ inside two \emph{separate}
pre-declared families of size 4, so harmful and benign sample sizes, rates,
$p$-values, and correction families are never combined. All displayed values are
those printed in \texttt{tables/llama33\_planner20\_primary\_results.tex}
(table \texttt{llama33\_planner20\_primary\_results}), with provenance recorded
in \texttt{handoffs/llama33\_planner20\_primary\_opus\_handoff.md} and
\texttt{manifests/llama33\_planner20\_primary\_artifact\_manifest.json}. These
results describe Phase~1 only and are not attributed to Phase~2 ``Turbo''.

In the harmful arm (harmful-arm columns of table
\texttt{llama33\_planner20\_primary\_results}, 4 language rows), planner-only
$\alpha20$ is associated with lower operational failure in Arabic, Hausa, and
Yoruba, and with essentially no change in English; all three reductions are
Holm-significant inside the harmful family of size 4 at family-wise
$\alpha = 0.05$. Arabic moves from 35.0 to 20.7 ($\Delta = -14.3$, 95\% CI
$[-21.0, -7.3]$, Holm $p = 0.000225$); Hausa moves from 68.3 to 58.3
($\Delta = -10.0$, CI $[-17.0, -3.0]$, Holm $p = 0.016762$); and Yoruba moves
from 74.7 to 58.0 ($\Delta = -16.7$, CI $[-22.3, -11.3]$, Holm $p < 0.001$).
English is essentially unchanged, from 13.7 to 13.3 ($\Delta = -0.3$, CI
$[-4.3, 3.7]$, Holm $p = 1.0$), and is not significant. The two largest
reductions occur in the two languages with the highest baseline failure rates
(Hausa 68.3 and Yoruba 74.7), while the language with the lowest baseline rate
shows no detectable change; the reported effect sizes and intervals are the only
quantities we attach to this pattern.

The benign arm (benign-arm columns 6--9 of the same table, 4 language rows)
records the benign refusal rate, for which a positive $\Delta$ means more false
refusals. Refusal rates are at or near zero throughout: Arabic 0.0 to 0.7
($\Delta = +0.7$, $p = 1.0$), English 0.0 to 0.0 ($\Delta = 0.0$, $p = 1.0$),
Hausa 0.0 to 1.3 ($\Delta = +1.3$, $p = 1.0$), and Yoruba 0.0 to 0.0
($\Delta = 0.0$, $p = 1.0$), with denominators 150, 150, 150, and 45
respectively. No cell in the benign family of size 4 is Holm-significant
(0 Holm-significant rows). These benign denominators, rates, and $p$-values
belong to their own correction family and are not merged with the harmful arm at
any point.

Taken together, the two arms show a prompt-matched within-model association
between planner-only $\alpha20$ steering and lower harmful operational failure
in Arabic, Hausa, and Yoruba, with no detectable change in English, alongside no
Holm-significant change in benign refusal rates. Several limits are binding on
this reading. The comparison is non-causal: it contrasts conditions on the same
prompts within one Phase~1 model entry and does not license claims about
mechanism or about behaviour under other deployment conditions. The absence of a
Holm-significant benign effect is not evidence that the intervention carries no
utility cost; the benign arm is small, has one partial-coverage row, and has only
45 Yoruba prompts, so it has limited power to detect small increases in false
refusals, especially for Yoruba, and we explicitly do not claim an absence of
cost for Yoruba. Finally, the token-level composition of the harmful failure
outcome---which decision tokens constitute a failure---is not documented in the
evidence base for this analysis, and missing-label handling is likewise not
established for it; we therefore make no statement about either, and we report no
parse-failure or missing-record counts for this model. The manifest additionally
lists non-shipped metric CSVs: \texttt{..\_paired\_effect.csv},
\texttt{..\_paired\_inference.csv}, \texttt{..\_primary\_paper\_table.csv}, and
\texttt{..\_primary\_raw\_separation\_audit.csv}. None of these files is cited as
evidence here.
