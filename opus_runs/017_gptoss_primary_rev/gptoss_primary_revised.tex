\subsection{GPT-OSS 20B harmful}

The fourth and final within-model question is whether planner-targeted steering
changes harmful decision outcomes in \texttt{openai/gpt-oss-20b}, and whether the
direction of any such change is consistent across languages. The contrast is the
Phase~1 unsteered baseline (no steering, $\alpha0$) against planner-only steering
at $\alpha1.0$, on prompt-matched harmful records only, with the outcome defined
as operational failure per the registered GPT-OSS decision-semantics and
parser-outcome audits (\texttt{registry/gpt\_oss\_decision\_semantics\_audit.json}
and \texttt{registry/gpt\_oss\_parser\_outcomes.json}). Those two registry audits
are not shipped in the evidence base, so the composition of the failure
outcome---which decision tokens compose it---is not independently verifiable
here, and the missing-label handling that they govern yields no counts we can
report. The analysis covers Arabic, English, Hausa, and Yoruba, harmful prompts
only, with 300 matched harmful prompts per language, 1{,}200 paired prompts in
total, and 0 partial-coverage rows. The canonical audit
(\texttt{metrics/gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_primary\_canonical\_audit.csv})
records 1{,}695 baseline rows (1{,}200 harmful plus 495 benign), 1{,}695
\texttt{planner\_only\_alpha1} rows, a paired harmful subset of 1{,}200, and 0
duplicate record IDs; the 495 benign rows per condition are not analysed here and
support no benign result. Each language cell is tested with the exact paired
two-sided McNemar test, and Holm correction is applied inside a family of four
language-specific harmful comparisons (size 4). Paired bootstrap intervals are
retained in a separate artifact that is not shipped, so we report per-cell
counts, rates, differences in percentage points, full discordance tables, raw
$p$-values, and Holm $p$-values, but no confidence intervals. The authoritative
source for inference is
\texttt{metrics/gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_paired\_effect\_holm.csv}
(4 rows), with effect and discordance quantities in
\texttt{metrics/gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_paired\_effect.csv}, the display
in
\texttt{tables/gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_primary\_harmful\_results.tex},
and provenance in
\texttt{handoffs/gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_primary\_harmful\_opus\_handoff.md}
and
\texttt{manifests/gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_primary\_harmful\_artifact\_manifest.json}.
The manifest additionally references
\texttt{gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_primary\_harmful\_paper\_table.csv} and
\texttt{gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_paired\_bootstrap\_ci.csv}, neither of
which is in the packet; neither is cited as evidence here.

The four language cells do not move in a common direction, and exactly two of
them are Holm-significant inside the four-comparison family. Arabic falls from
43 of 300 (14.33\%) to 30 of 300 (10.00\%), $\Delta = -4.33$~pp, with 33
discordant prompts (10 worsened, 23 improved), raw $p = 0.03508$ and Holm
$p = 0.070164$: the raw $p$-value falls below 0.05 while the family-corrected
decision does not reject, and we report the two jointly, so Arabic is
\emph{not} significant. English falls from 174 of 300 (58.00\%) to 141 of 300
(47.00\%), $\Delta = -11.0$~pp, with 105 discordant prompts (36 worsened, 69
improved), raw $p = 0.0016590$ and Holm $p = 0.004977$, and is significant.
Hausa moves in the opposite direction, rising from 45 of 300 (15.00\%) to 66 of
300 (22.00\%), $\Delta = +7.0$~pp---a \emph{worsening} of harmful operational
failure under steering---with 27 discordant prompts distributed asymmetrically
(24 worsened, 3 improved), raw $p = 4.92334\times10^{-5}$ and Holm
$p = 0.00019693$, and is significant. Yoruba is exactly unchanged at 48 of 300
(16.00\%) in both conditions, $\Delta = 0.0$, with 36 discordant prompts split
evenly (18 worsened and 18 improved) and $p = 1.0$. Thus, the equal marginal
rates coexist with matched prompt-level disagreements in both directions.
Across the family, then, one language improves significantly, one worsens
significantly, one shows a point reduction that does not survive Holm
correction, and one shows no marginal change at all.

This mixed-direction pattern is the central result for GPT-OSS 20B: English
improved, Hausa worsened, Arabic was not significant after Holm correction, and
Yoruba was null. Several limits bind its reading. The comparison estimates a
condition-specific paired difference on the evaluated prompts: it contrasts two
conditions on the same harmful prompts within a single Phase~1 model entry, and
it does not establish a general causal effect across deployment settings, model
revisions, prompt distributions, or outcome definitions. It licenses no
mechanistic claim, no claim about behaviour under other deployment conditions,
and no claim that planner-only steering produces an improvement in general. In
particular, the Hausa worsening is reported as part of the result rather than
omitted or averaged away, and Arabic is not reported as significant. Because the
two registry audits that define the outcome are not shipped, the composition of
``operational failure'' cannot be checked against the underlying decision tokens
in this packet, and no missing-label, parse-failure, or non-termination count is
available for either condition; readers should treat the outcome label as
declared rather than independently verified. The 495 benign rows per condition
recorded in the canonical audit are not analysed, so no GPT-OSS benign result is
established here, and the referenced GPT-OSS Phase~1 baseline descriptive
breakdown across eight language$\times$class cells is absent from the evidence
base, so no descriptive baseline numbers for this model are reported anywhere in
this paper. Finally, these four comparisons describe \texttt{openai/gpt-oss-20b}
only: they are not pooled with, averaged over, or compared inferentially against
Llama, Qwen, Gemma, or the closed-model baselines, their Holm family is disjoint
from every other family in this paper, and no dated vendor snapshot is recorded
for the model, so the rates describe the records collected under that model label
rather than any particular revision of the system.
