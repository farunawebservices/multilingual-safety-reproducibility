\subsection{GPT-5 frontier baseline}

Having reported the four within-model steering comparisons, we turn to the
closed-model baselines, whose role in this paper is descriptive only: they
record how a frontier system behaves on the same multilingual prompt set, with
no intervention arm of any kind. The question asked here is therefore not
whether steering changes outcomes, but simply what the per-cell veto and
non-veto rates of \texttt{openai/gpt-5} are. The analysis uses Phase~1 records
under a single configuration, \texttt{frontier\_baseline} at strength 0.0; there
is no steered arm, no second condition, and consequently pairing is not
applicable. The cells are Arabic, English, Hausa, and Yoruba crossed with benign
and harmful prompt classes. Two outcomes are recorded per cell: the veto rate,
defined as \texttt{is\_vetoed = true}, and the non-veto operational failure
rate, defined as \texttt{is\_vetoed = false}. Benign denominators are
150/150/150/45 and harmful denominators are 286/291/289/290, over 1{,}651 valid
unique-ID records, with 5 partial-coverage rows (the 4 harmful cells plus Yoruba
benign). The harmful denominators are \emph{not} 300, and we report them exactly
as recorded rather than as a nominal target. All displayed values are those
printed in \texttt{metrics/gpt\_5\_frontier\_baseline\_paper\_table.csv} (table
\texttt{gpt\_5\_frontier\_baseline\_paper\_table}, 8 cells) and
\texttt{tables/gpt\_5\_frontier\_baseline\_descriptive\_results.tex}, with
decision composition in
\texttt{metrics/gpt\_5\_frontier\_baseline\_canonical\_audit.csv} (whose
filename differs from the manifest-listed
\texttt{gpt\_5\_clean\_frontier\_baseline\_audit.csv}) and provenance in
\texttt{handoffs/gpt\_5\_frontier\_baseline\_OPUS\_HANDOFF.md} and
\texttt{manifests/gpt\_5\_frontier\_baseline\_artifact\_manifest.json}. Because
there is a single condition, no test, no confidence interval, and no
multiple-testing correction is defined for or applied to any cell in this
subsection.

In the four benign cells, vetoes are essentially absent. Arabic benign records
0 of 150 vetoes (0.00\% veto, 100.00\% non-veto), Hausa benign 0 of 150, and
Yoruba benign 0 of 45; English benign records 3 of 150 (2.00\% veto, 98.00\%
non-veto). In the four harmful cells, veto rates are substantially higher and
vary by language: Arabic harmful 233 of 286 (81.47\% veto, 18.53\% non-veto),
English harmful 178 of 291 (61.17\%, 38.83\%), Hausa harmful 214 of 289
(74.05\%, 25.95\%), and Yoruba harmful 204 of 290 (70.34\%, 29.66\%). Each rate
is stated against its own denominator, and the harmful denominators are 286,
291, 289, and 290 respectively rather than 300; the shortfall relative to 300 is
not a parsing or termination artefact. The canonical audit reports
\texttt{parse\_failure\_n = 0} and \texttt{non\_termination\_n = 0} in all 8
cells, so the harmful shortfall consists of absent records, not invalid ones,
and no record is dropped here on validity grounds. We therefore report no
missing-label imputation and no parse-failure count other than zero, and we do
not rescale any cell to a 300-prompt denominator.

The interpretation of these eight cells is bounded in several ways. They are
descriptive: no hypothesis test, interval, or correction family is attached to
them, and no statement in this subsection may be read as inferential. They are
also non-comparative. In particular, the GPT-5 rates are not contrasted with the
steered open-weight arms of Section~4, are not pooled with any other model
entry, and are not used to rank models; the single \texttt{frontier\_baseline}
condition at strength 0.0 supports no within-model effect estimate because there
is no second arm to compare it against, and the between-language differences
among the harmful cells are reported as observed rates only. A further data-hygiene
constraint is binding: a separate 570-record file exists for this model, and it
is an exact duplicate subset of the analysed records---570 shared IDs, 570
matching language, harmfulness, and veto outcomes, and 1 parse error. That file
is not pooled with, appended to, or used to supplement the 1{,}651 valid
unique-ID records reported above, and none of its counts enters any denominator
here. Finally, the analysis identifies the model only by the string
\texttt{openai/gpt-5}; no vendor snapshot or dated model version is recorded in
the evidence base, so these rates describe the records collected under that
configuration and are not claimed to characterise any particular vendor snapshot
or any later or earlier revision of the system.
