\section{Experimental setup}

All analyses in this paper are defined at the level of individual prompts
evaluated under two or more conditions for a fixed model. In each steering
analysis, the primary estimand is the change in a binary decision outcome---an
operational failure or a refusal---between a steered condition and an unsteered
baseline, measured on the same prompts. All open-weight model comparisons are
within-model and prompt-matched; the Llama cross-phase role/strength analysis is
additionally record-ID-matched but explicitly associational; and the Claude
comparison is a descriptive cross-version contrast with no steering arm. No
result in this paper pools outcomes across different models.

\subsection{Models and phases}

Six model entries define the analysis units, and every analysis is reported
within a single model entry; no result is pooled across models. The open-weight
entries are \texttt{meta-llama/llama-3.3-70b-instruct}, Qwen 2.5 72B
(\texttt{qwen25\_72b}), \texttt{gemma\_4\_31b}, and
\texttt{openai/gpt-oss-20b}; the closed-model baselines are
\texttt{openai/gpt-5} and Claude Sonnet~4 together with Claude Sonnet~5.
For Llama~3.3~70B, the primary harmful and benign analyses are Phase~1
(``main'') only, and Phase~2 ``Turbo'' is explicitly excluded from them; the
separate role/strength analysis contrasts Phase~1 main $\alpha0$ against
Phase~2 Turbo $\alpha30$ and is labelled cross-phase throughout. Qwen 2.5 72B,
\texttt{gemma\_4\_31b} (Phase~1 complete-case), \texttt{openai/gpt-oss-20b}
(Phase~1), \texttt{openai/gpt-5} (Phase~1 records, configuration
\texttt{frontier\_baseline}, strength 0.0), and the Claude Sonnet~4 versus
Sonnet~5 comparison (Phase~1 shared prompts) are all single-phase. Phase and
condition labels follow
\texttt{handoffs/llama33\_planner20\_primary\_opus\_handoff.md},
\texttt{handoffs/llama33\_turbo\_alpha30\_OPUS\_HANDOFF.md},
\texttt{handoffs/qwen25\_72b\_planner\_alpha1\_primary\_opus\_handoff.md}, and
the manifests
\texttt{manifests/llama33\_planner20\_primary\_artifact\_manifest.json} and
\texttt{manifests/qwen25\_72b\_planner\_alpha1\_primary\_artifact\_manifest.json}.

Three qualifications on model identity and phase comparability are binding. The
Qwen, Gemma, and Claude entries carry no vendor path or snapshot identifier in
the evidence base: Qwen is recorded only as ``Qwen 2.5 72B''/\texttt{qwen25\_72b},
\texttt{gemma\_4\_31b} has no vendor identifier or snapshot recorded anywhere,
and the Claude entries are recorded only as ``Claude Sonnet 4'' and ``Claude
Sonnet 5'' without vendor API strings or snapshot dates. Second, the Llama
cross-phase analysis is record-ID-matched but pairing alone does not establish
non-prompt run-condition equivalence, so we do not claim that Phase~1 and
Phase~2 run conditions were equivalent, and we do not attribute the Phase~1
planner-only results to Phase~2 Turbo. Third, the Claude analysis is a single
joint cross-version comparison: no standalone Sonnet~4 or Sonnet~5 analysis
exists in the evidence base, and none is reported here.

\subsection{Prompt sets and languages}

The prompt set spans Arabic, English, Hausa, and Yoruba, crossed with benign and
harmful prompt classes. Yoruba benign has $n = 45$ in every analysis in which it
appears, and harmful cells are 300 prompts per language wherever pairing is
complete. Per-model denominators are reported exactly as printed in their source
artifacts and are never summed across arms or models. For Llama~3.3~70B
(\texttt{tables/llama33\_planner20\_primary\_results.tex}, table
\texttt{llama33\_planner20\_primary\_results}), the harmful arm has 300
prompt-matched prompts per language, 1{,}200 in total, with 0 partial
harmful-coverage rows. The benign arm has denominators 150/150/150/45,
495 in total, with 1 partial benign-coverage row. The harmful total of 1{,}200
and the benign total of 495 must not be combined. The Llama cross-phase
role/strength analysis
(\texttt{metrics/llama33\_turbo\_alpha30\_role\_strength\_unified\_results.csv},
6 rows) covers Hausa and Yoruba only, with 300 matched harmful prompts per cell
and 1{,}800 in total.

For Qwen 2.5 72B, the primary planner-only $\alpha1.0$ table has 8
language$\times$class cells with benign 150/150/150/45 and harmful
300/300/300/300, 1{,}695 paired prompts and 0 partial-coverage rows. The wider
grid in \texttt{metrics/qwen25\_72b\_primary\_unified\_results.csv} (33 rows)
contains three partial-coverage cells whose denominators are preserved verbatim:
\texttt{critic\_only} Arabic benign $\alpha1.0$ with steered $n = 6$ against
baseline 150 (\texttt{partial\_baseline\_coverage = true}); \texttt{both} Yoruba
harmful $\alpha2.0$ with paired $n = 160$ (baseline 300, 140 baseline-only); and
\texttt{planner\_only} Yoruba harmful $\alpha2.0$ with paired $n = 173$ (127
baseline-only). These denominators are neither rounded up nor silently dropped.
For \texttt{gemma\_4\_31b}
(\texttt{metrics/gemma\_4\_31b\_phase1\_complete\_case\_unified\_results.csv},
3 rows), only three cells exist: Arabic benign (150 paired), English benign
(101 paired; baseline 101, steered 112, 11 steered-only valid IDs excluded,
\texttt{partial\_coverage = true}), and Arabic harmful (300 paired). There
are no Hausa, Yoruba, or English harmful cells, and no total is recorded, so the
three denominators are not summed.

For the closed-model baselines, \texttt{openai/gpt-5}
(\texttt{metrics/gpt\_5\_frontier\_baseline\_paper\_table.csv}, 8 cells) has
benign denominators 150/150/150/45 and harmful denominators 286/291/289/290 over
1{,}651 valid unique-ID records, with 5 partial-coverage rows (4 harmful cells
plus Yoruba benign); the harmful denominators are not 300. The canonical audit
records \texttt{parse\_failure\_n = 0} and \texttt{non\_termination\_n = 0} in all
8 cells, so the harmful shortfall consists of absent records rather than invalid
ones. The Claude cross-version comparison
(\texttt{metrics/claude\_sonnet\_4\_vs\_5\_outcome\_comparison.csv}, 8 rows,
with \texttt{metrics/claude\_sonnet\_4\_vs\_5\_metadata\_consistency\_audit.csv},
1 row) uses 1{,}695 shared prompt IDs, benign 150/150/150/45 and harmful 300 per
language, with 1 partial-coverage row (Yoruba benign, 45 shared prompts); the
metadata audit reports 1{,}695 records present in both versions with language and
harmfulness matching for all. For \texttt{openai/gpt-oss-20b}, the harmful
analysis uses 300 matched prompts per language, 1{,}200 paired, and 0
partial-coverage rows. The canonical audit
(\texttt{metrics/gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_primary\_canonical\_audit.csv})
records baseline 1{,}695 rows (1{,}200 harmful plus 495 benign),
\texttt{planner\_only\_alpha1} 1{,}695 rows, a paired harmful subset of 1{,}200,
and 0 duplicate record IDs. A GPT-OSS Phase~1 baseline descriptive breakdown
across 8 language$\times$class cells is referenced in the evidence base but its
artifacts are absent from the packet, so no numbers from it are reported
anywhere in this paper.

\subsection{Interventions and configurations}

The intervention grid is recorded per model and is reported exactly as declared.
For Llama~3.3~70B, the Phase~1 contrast is planner-only activation steering at
$\alpha20$ against a planner-only $\alpha0$ baseline; the separate cross-phase
contrast compares Phase~1 main $\alpha0$ against Phase~2 Turbo $\alpha30$ under
three role configurations, \texttt{planner\_only}, \texttt{critic\_only}, and
\texttt{both}, giving 3 configurations $\times$ 2 languages $=$ 6 cells in the
authoritative
\texttt{metrics/llama33\_turbo\_alpha30\_role\_strength\_unified\_results.csv}.
For Qwen 2.5 72B, the baseline $\alpha0$ condition is compared against
\texttt{planner\_only}, \texttt{critic\_only}, and \texttt{both} at $\alpha1.0$
and $\alpha2.0$ (\texttt{metrics/qwen25\_72b\_primary\_paired\_inference.csv}),
with planner-only $\alpha1.0$ as the primary displayed configuration. Gemma
compares baseline $\alpha0$ against planner-only $\alpha1.0$
(\texttt{metrics/gemma\_4\_31b\_phase1\_complete\_case\_paired\_inference.csv}),
and GPT-OSS 20B compares an unsteered baseline ($\alpha0$) against planner-only
$\alpha1.0$
(\texttt{metrics/gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_paired\_effect.csv}).

The two closed-model analyses contain no intervention arm. GPT-5 is a single
configuration, \texttt{frontier\_baseline} at strength 0.0
(\texttt{tables/gpt\_5\_frontier\_baseline\_descriptive\_results.tex}), so
pairing is not applicable. The Claude analysis contrasts two model versions with
no steering condition on either side. Three limits on the intervention
description are binding. No random-vector or matched-control condition was run
in any analysis reported here, and none is claimed. The Llama cross-phase
contrast varies role configuration and strength jointly with phase and therefore
does not isolate role specificity or vector specificity; the original steering
vector file is declared unavailable, and we make no claim to the contrary.
Finally, the defective artifacts
\texttt{metrics/llama33\_turbo\_alpha30\_paper\_table.csv} and
\texttt{tables/llama33\_turbo\_alpha30\_cross\_phase\_comparison.tex} are not
cited as evidence anywhere.

\subsection{Outcome definitions and decision semantics}

Outcome definitions are model-specific and are not interchangeable. For the
Llama cross-phase analysis, harmful operational failure is defined as explicit
ALLOW plus NO\_DECISION, with NO\_DECISION folded into the failure numerator, and
no separate parse-failure or missing count is provided. For the Llama Phase~1
planner-only analysis, the outcome is the harmful operational failure rate
reported in the audited metric CSV and LaTeX table; however, the token-level
composition of that outcome---which decision tokens constitute failure---is not
documented in the evidence base, so we make no statement about it. The Llama
Phase~1 benign outcome is a distinct refusal rate, for which a positive $\Delta$
means more false refusals. For Qwen 2.5 72B, an explicit veto counts as safe and
a non-veto unresolved record counts as operational failure; non-veto records are
not assumed to be explicit ALLOW. For \texttt{gemma\_4\_31b}, non-veto
operational failure is \texttt{is\_vetoed = false} under a complete-case
valid-JSON policy in which one truncated terminal JSONL record is excluded from
each source and missing outcomes are not imputed. This Gemma metric is not an
explicit-ALLOW rate. The Gemma benign rows are not described as safety failures
and are not comparable to the Llama benign refusal rates, since Gemma has no
separate benign-utility refusal outcome. For \texttt{openai/gpt-5} and for
the Claude cross-version comparison, the outcome is veto status
(\texttt{is\_vetoed = true}), with the GPT-5 non-veto operational failure rate
defined as \texttt{is\_vetoed = false}.

Decision semantics are documented by per-model audits. The Qwen audit
(\texttt{metrics/qwen25\_72b\_primary\_canonical\_audit.csv}, 41 rows across the
\texttt{baseline}, \texttt{planner\_steered}, and
\texttt{critic\_and\_both\_steered} source groups) is purely descriptive and
reports veto/non-veto composition, critic token counts, record-ID uniqueness,
and definition-validity flags; no hypothesis test, interval, or multiple-testing
statement is attached to it. The corresponding Gemma audit is
\texttt{metrics/gemma\_4\_31b\_phase1\_complete\_case\_audit.csv} (its filename
differs from the manifest-listed
\texttt{..\_decision\_semantics\_audit.csv}), and the GPT-5 audit is
\texttt{metrics/gpt\_5\_frontier\_baseline\_canonical\_audit.csv} (filename
differs from the manifest-listed
\texttt{gpt\_5\_clean\_frontier\_baseline\_audit.csv}). For GPT-OSS 20B, the
cell-level counts come from
\texttt{metrics/gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_primary\_canonical\_audit.csv},
but the outcome composition depends on registry decision-semantics and
parser-outcome audits that are not shipped. That composition is therefore not
independently verifiable here, and no missing-label count is available.
Missing-label handling is likewise not established for the Llama Phase~1 and
cross-phase analyses, for Qwen, or for the Claude comparison, and we do not
report one. The only positive missing-data statements we make are the Gemma
complete-case exclusion policy and the GPT-5 audit's
\texttt{parse\_failure\_n = 0} and \texttt{non\_termination\_n = 0} in all 8
cells.

\subsection{Statistical procedures and correction families}

All inferential analyses use the exact paired two-sided McNemar test on
prompt-matched or record-ID-matched pairs. Paired nonparametric bootstrap
percentile 95\% confidence intervals with 10{,}000 replicates and seed 20260908
accompany the Llama, Qwen, and Gemma analyses; for GPT-OSS 20B the paired
bootstrap intervals are retained in a separate artifact that is not shipped, so
GPT-OSS results are reported with McNemar $p$-values and discordance counts
only. Multiple-testing correction is Holm within pre-declared, mutually disjoint
per-model families, at family-wise $\alpha = 0.05$: Llama harmful, size 4;
Llama benign, a \emph{separate} family of size 4; the Llama cross-phase family
\texttt{turbo\_alpha30\_role\_strength\_6\_tests}, size 6
(\texttt{metrics/llama33\_turbo\_alpha30\_role\_strength\_paired\_inference.csv});
the Qwen pre-declared family \texttt{qwen25\_72b\_primary\_33\_steered\_cells},
size 33
(\texttt{metrics/qwen25\_72b\_primary\_paired\_inference\_holm.csv}, 33 rows);
the Gemma family \texttt{gemma\_phase1\_three\_cell\_family}, size 3
(\texttt{metrics/gemma\_4\_31b\_phase1\_complete\_case\_paired\_inference\_holm.csv},
3 rows); and the GPT-OSS family of four language-specific harmful comparisons,
size 4
(\texttt{metrics/gpt\_oss\_20b\_planner\_alpha1\_paired\_effect\_holm.csv},
4 rows). The Llama harmful and benign families are never combined, and Holm
significance for Qwen is reported only within the 33-cell family.

Three analyses are descriptive and carry no test, interval, or correction: the
GPT-5 frontier baseline, the Claude Sonnet~4 versus Sonnet~5 cross-version
comparison, and the Qwen 41-cell canonical audit. In particular, the Qwen and
Gemma Holm families are not applied to or reported for the Claude comparison,
which is explicitly non-causal and is not a within-model steering effect. Where
interval-based and Holm-based conclusions diverge, both are reported jointly and
are not conflated: a confidence interval excluding zero is not reported as a
Holm rejection. All inferential claims in this paper are non-causal
prompt-matched comparisons; the Llama cross-phase role/strength analysis is
additionally associational, since matching by record ID does not establish
non-prompt run-condition equivalence between Phase~1 and Phase~2. Accordingly,
all reported effects should be interpreted as descriptive or associational
differences between conditions on the same prompts, not as evidence that
steering causes changes in behaviour under arbitrary deployment conditions.
