%TL;DR: Multilingual activation steering should be evaluated as a conditional pipeline intervention, not as a model-wide safety switch.
\section{Conclusion}
We evaluated activation steering at different roles in a multilingual Planner--Tool-Caller--Critic safety pipeline. The results showed strong conditionality: in a cross-phase Llama role-strength comparison, Planner-only steering reduced harmful operational failure in Hausa and Yoruba, Critic-only steering increased it, and joint steering had opposite effects across the two languages. Primary within-model analyses across four open-weight entries likewise showed improvements in some model--language cells, null effects in others, and a significant worsening in one GPT-OSS language.

The main methodological lesson is that aggregate safety rates are insufficient for structured-output pipelines. Evaluations should preserve prompt pairing, language identity, intervention role, exact denominators, parser validity, and the composition of explicit VETO, explicit ALLOW, and no-decision outcomes. Future work should use same-phase factorial designs, matched random-vector controls, repeated decoding, and reconstructed full-context causal patching to determine which parts of these behavioral contrasts arise from vector specificity, semantic judgment, or generation stability.
