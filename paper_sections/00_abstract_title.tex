\title{Where You Intervene Matters: Role- and Language-Dependent Effects of Activation Steering in Multilingual Safety Pipelines}

\begin{abstract}
Activation steering is increasingly used to modify language-model behavior, but its effects in multilingual multi-agent safety pipelines may depend on where the intervention is applied. We evaluate Planner-only, Critic-only, and joint steering in a Planner--Tool-Caller--Critic architecture across multiple languages and open-weight models. Our prompt-matched analyses use exact paired McNemar tests, paired bootstrap intervals, and predeclared Holm correction families. In a cross-phase, record-ID-matched Llama 3.3 70B role-strength analysis, alpha-30 Planner-only steering reduced harmful operational failure by 37.7 percentage points in Hausa and 19.3 points in Yoruba, whereas Critic-only steering increased failure by 6.3 and 13.0 points. Joint steering improved Hausa by 19.0 points but worsened Yoruba by 10.0 points. Decision-composition analysis showed that these contrasts coincided with large changes in explicit VETO, explicit ALLOW, and unresolved no-decision outputs, including a 99.3\% no-decision rate for Yoruba under Critic-only alpha 20. In canonical within-model primary analyses, Planner-only steering improved Arabic and English outcomes for Qwen 2.5 72B, while effects were smaller or non-significant in Hausa and Yoruba; Llama, Gemma, and GPT-OSS likewise showed heterogeneous patterns. We conclude that multilingual steering should be evaluated by role, language, outcome composition, and interface validity rather than summarized by a single model-level safety score.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Keywords.} activation steering, multilingual safety, multi-agent systems, evaluation, output validity, language-model alignment.
