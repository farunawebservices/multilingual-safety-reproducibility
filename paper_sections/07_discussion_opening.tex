\section{Discussion}

Across the four open-weight model entries evaluated here, planner-targeted
steering is associated with changes in multilingual decision outcomes whose
magnitude and direction depend jointly on the model, the language, and the
outcome definition. In \texttt{meta-llama/Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct} (Phase~1,
planner-only $\alpha20$ vs.\ $\alpha0$), harmful operational failure is
substantially lower under steering in Arabic, Hausa, and Yoruba, with all three
reductions surviving Holm correction inside the pre-declared four-comparison
harmful family, while English---the language with the lowest baseline failure
rate---is essentially unchanged. In Qwen~2.5~72B (baseline vs.\ planner-only
$\alpha1.0$), the two Holm-significant cells of the primary display are both
harmful cells, Arabic and English, with the largest observed reduction in
English harmful; the Hausa and Yoruba harmful point reductions do not survive
correction inside the pre-declared 33-cell family, and no benign cell is
Holm-significant at any role or strength. In GPT-OSS~20B the pattern is
mixed in direction rather than uniformly favourable: English improved
significantly, Hausa \emph{worsened} significantly, Arabic showed a point
reduction that did not survive Holm correction, and Yoruba showed no marginal
change at all despite prompt-level disagreements in both directions. In the three
Gemma cells for which paired records exist, all point estimates move in the
direction of \emph{more} non-veto failure under steering, but no cell is
Holm-significant inside the three-cell family, so the consistent direction is an
observed pattern in point estimates and intervals rather than a family-corrected
finding. On the utility side, the Llama benign refusal family shows no
Holm-significant change, with refusal at or near the floor in every language;
this is consistent with, but does not establish, an absence of utility cost.

The closed-model analyses play a purely contextual role and should not be read as
evidence about steering. The \texttt{openai/gpt-5} analysis is a single-condition
descriptive baseline at strength $0.0$ with no intervention arm: it records how a
frontier system behaves on the same multilingual prompt set, with veto rates that
are near-zero on benign prompts and substantially higher, and language-varying,
on harmful prompts. Because there is no second arm, it supports no within-model
effect estimate, and we do not contrast it with the steered open-weight arms or
use it to rank systems. The Claude Sonnet~4 vs.~5 analysis is likewise
descriptive: it is prompt-matched across shared Phase~1 prompt IDs, but the
contrast is between two model \emph{versions} rather than two conditions of one
model, carries no test or correction family, and identifies nothing about what
produced the observed differences. Its main value here is to record that
outcome-relevant behaviour can shift across closed-model versions on identical
prompts---a background source of variation that any steering evaluation
conducted against closed baselines inherits.

Two structural features of the evidence constrain how much can be concluded from
the pattern above. First, each open-weight model carries its own disjoint
correction family---Llama harmful, Llama benign, the Qwen pre-declared 33-cell
family, the Gemma three-cell family, and the four-comparison GPT-OSS harmful
family---and these families are never merged, pooled, or averaged. Every
statement of significance is therefore internal to one model entry, one outcome,
and one family; there is no family-corrected cross-model contrast in this paper,
and the apparent similarity between, say, the Llama and Qwen reductions is a
juxtaposition of separate analyses rather than a joint estimate. Second, the
additional analyses of Section~6 (Additions~1--6) are exploratory and secondary:
they declare no correction family, reuse no primary claim identifier, and are
reported to document what the shipped artifacts contain and to motivate future
pre-registered designs, not to support conclusions. In several of them the
required artifact is simply absent---most consequentially, no matched
random-vector control on the same prompts and outcome definition exists anywhere
in the evidence base, so generic perturbation is neither identified nor ruled
out, and vector specificity is not established by any analysis reported here.

For steering design, the most defensible reading is a conditional one. Within the
configurations we evaluated, planner-only steering was associated with large
reductions in harmful operational failure in some model$\times$language cells,
including cells with high baseline failure rates in lower-resource languages
(Hausa and Yoruba for Llama), and with essentially no movement where baseline
failure was already low. This is consistent with treating the intervention
\emph{location} within an agentic pipeline as a design variable worth measuring
rather than assuming. At the same time, the effects are not uniform and are not
guaranteed to be beneficial: the same nominal intervention type worsened harmful
outcomes in one language of one model (Hausa in GPT-OSS), produced only
uncorrected point reductions elsewhere, and was associated with point-estimate
increases in non-veto failure in all available Gemma cells. Any deployment-facing
use of planner-targeted steering therefore requires per-model, per-language
measurement with its own pre-declared correction family and its own benign-side
outcome; a favourable result in one language or model entry provides no warrant
for another. We also emphasise that none of the comparisons reported here is
causal or mechanistic: they contrast conditions on matched prompts within single
model entries, and they license no claim about why outcomes moved, nor about
behaviour under other prompt distributions, deployment settings, or outcome
definitions.

\subsection{Limitations}

Several limitations bound every claim above. Outcome definitions are
heterogeneous across model entries---harmful operational failure, non-veto
operational failure, veto rate, benign refusal rate, and various
Addition-specific outcomes such as endpoint ALLOW/VETO decisions, harmful VETO
rate, jailbreak success, and annotation agreement---so the rates are not on a
common scale and are not comparable across models even when they carry similar
labels; relatedly, for several models the decision-semantics and parser audits
that define the outcome are not shipped in the evidence base, so the composition
of ``failure'' or ``refusal'' cannot be independently verified here, and
missing-label, parse-failure, and non-termination counts are unavailable
(readers should treat those outcome labels as declared rather than checked).
Coverage is partial in several cells and must be read against the exact recorded
denominators rather than nominal targets: the Yoruba benign arm is small
throughout, the Gemma analysis exists for only three cells with one
reduced-coverage cell and no recorded total, the Qwen family contains three
truncated steered cells, the GPT-5 harmful cells fall short of the nominal
denominator, and Addition~6 records markedly asymmetric missing-label counts
across its three conditions so that its condition denominators are not
label-complete and its differences are unadjusted for that asymmetry. No dated
vendor snapshot is recorded for any model entry, and some entries carry no vendor
identifier at all, which limits both reproducibility and temporal
generalisation: the reported rates describe the records collected under the given
model labels rather than any particular revision of the underlying systems, and
the Claude cross-version comparison illustrates directly that behaviour on
identical prompts can shift across versions. Additions~1--6 are exploratory, have
no correction families, report no confirmatory tests, and in some cases lack the
numeric artifacts their intended analysis would require; cross-phase comparisons
are further confounded because run phase, steering strength, and configuration
label vary together and record-ID matching does not establish non-prompt
run-condition equivalence. Finally, and most importantly, the analyses support
model-specific, language-specific, and condition-specific descriptions only. They
do not license broad claims about ``steering'' as a technique, about safety in
languages or prompt classes we did not evaluate, or about deployment settings,
model revisions, and outcome definitions outside those recorded here; absence of
a Holm-significant effect in any family is likewise not evidence of absence,
particularly in the small, floor-valued benign arms.
